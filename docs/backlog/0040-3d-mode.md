## Why
The operator asked for a 3D mode. The 2D canvas is a pseudo-3D road (projected
segments); a true 3D view (camera you can feel, depth, glowing neon geometry, cars with
volume) makes the synthwave fantasy land on tablets and laptops.

## What
- Split: the simulation must not read anything the renderer writes. The tyre contact Z
  (`visualPlayerZ`) moves from `renderScene()` to `src/contact.js` (pure, tested).
- `src/render3d/`: a lazy-loaded three.js view of the same `GameState`, `track` and
  traffic bodies. The 2D bundle stays within its budget; the 3D chunk has its own.
- Toggle 2D/3D on the title and in the garage/settings, persisted; auto 2D when WebGL is
  missing or the device is low-end; context loss falls back to 2D.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Simulation identical in 2D and 3D (contact Z equivalence test, no render writes)
- [ ] 3D boots under SwiftShader, counts down, renders frames, zero console errors
- [ ] Survives tablet rotation (768x1024 ↔ 1024x768) in 3D
- [ ] 2D main bundle ≤ 60 kB gzipped; 3D chunk within its documented budget

## Test plan
`tests/unit/contact.test.js` (legacy-renderer equivalence), `tests/unit/render-mode.test.js`
(mode choice + persistence), `tests/unit/scene-model.test.js` (track → 3D path),
`tests/e2e/mode3d.spec.js`.

## Out of scope
Changing physics, AI or race rules; heavy post-processing.
